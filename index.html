ACT Status Checks Before Major Tree Work

A crucial aspect of tree management in the ACT involves confirming the tree's ownership and protected status. The source guide clarifies that all public trees are automatically protected. Furthermore, a private tree can also be subject to protection under the Urban Forest Act 2023, potentially due to its size, registration status, or other qualifying conditions. Even a dead native tree may still require a careful status check before any action is taken.

For private trees, the guide directs property owners to consult current City Services guidelines and utilise ACTmapi for status verification before arranging major work. For public trees, it is explicitly stated that residents must not undertake pruning or removal themselves. This information is vital for shaping the initial conversation with a potential service provider. Key questions include:

  • Has the ownership of the tree (private or public land) been definitively confirmed?
  • Has the protected status of the tree been thoroughly checked using current ACT guidance?
  • Does the proposed work scope assume that an approval process will be necessary?

If the protected status of a tree is uncertain, the recommended approach is not to immediately request a price. Instead, inquire about the specific evidence the provider requires to assess the feasibility of the work, whether an arborist assessment would be beneficial, and what information needs to be gathered before any removal or significant pruning can be authorised.

What Changes the Written Scope of Work

Achieving a useful comparison of quotes relies on each service provider responding to the exact same job specifications. The source guide advocates for clearly documenting the proposed method, necessary approvals, provider credentials, insurance details, and all inclusions and exclusions in writing. This transforms the selection process from a simple lowest-price exercise into a comprehensive comparison of what is actually being offered.

Several practical variables, often visible on-site, can significantly impact the scope and cost of tree work:

  • Access Width: Narrow access may necessitate different equipment or removal techniques, potentially increasing complexity.
  • Nearby Assets: Proximity to roofs, fences, and underground or overhead services requires careful rigging and protective measures.
  • Stump Work: The scope must explicitly detail whether stump grinding is included, what will be done with the resulting grindings, and if backfilling the area is expected.
  • Waste Disposal: Different arrangements can be made for logs, mulch, and general green waste. It is essential that these are clearly itemised in the quote, as assumptions should not be made.

For pruning tasks, the guide promotes selective work focused on specific objectives like deadwood removal, clearance, or canopy management, rather than indiscriminate lopping, which can harm the tree's health and structure.

Key Questions Before Accepting a Quote

The most effective questions to ask are those that are specific to the particular tree and site conditions, rather than relying on generic assurances. It is advisable to inquire about:

  • Whether the provider has received sufficient photographic evidence to accurately comment on the proposed method.
  • How potential public or protected tree issues might influence the work plan.
  • Whether stump grinding, green waste removal, and site clean-up are explicitly included in the quote.
  • Confirmation of land ownership (private or public).
  • Verification of protected tree status using current ACT guidance and ACTmapi.
  • The specific type of work proposed: removal, pruning, assessment, stump grinding, or urgent make-safe services.
  • Any site constraints, such as nearby assets, visible lines, slope, or access limitations, that could affect the method.
  • The proof of qualifications, insurance, and availability that the actual operator will provide.

If any answers are uncertain, it is best to have that uncertainty clearly documented within the written scope. A clearly stated exclusion is far preferable to discovering after accepting a quote that essential services like stump grinding, green waste removal, access repair, or obtaining necessary approvals were never part of the original offer.
